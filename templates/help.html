The idea

Entropicide follows Getting Things Done (GTD): get everything out of your head into a trusted place, decide what each thing is, and then work from short lists instead of one long one.

  1. Capture anything the moment it comes up. It lands in the Inbox; don't sort it yet.
  2. Clarify each Inbox item: is it something to do? What's the very next physical step? Is it part of a bigger outcome (a project)?
  3. Organize it into a list: the Queue if you'll do it as soon as you can, Soon or Later if not yet, Waiting if someone else has it, Scheduled if it can't start until a date, Someday if maybe never.
  4. Do: work from Focus (today) and the Queue.
  5. Review weekly so the lists stay trustworthy (see routine).

The lists

Every task is in exactly one list (its status), plus it can be starred. Change the list from the task's details, by dragging it onto a list in the sidebar, by right-clicking → Move to, or with !list when adding it.

Inbox
Where new things land. It should be emptied often (daily is good): it's a collecting point, not a place to keep things.
“Look into solar panels” → later decide: Someday? A project?
★ Focus
What you're doing today. It shows starred tasks plus anything due today or overdue, so nothing due slips by. Star a task (☆ on its row, right-click → Star, !focus, or drag it onto Focus) to pull it into today's list; unstar it if the day changes. Tasks in Someday or Later, or in a completed project, don't show here. Sorted by priority.
Morning: star the 3–5 Queue tasks you mean to finish today.
Queue
GTD's next actions: things you could do as soon as you have the time, each a concrete step. This is your main working list. Sorted by priority, then your drag order.
“Email Ana the budget draft”, not “Budget”.
Soon
Things to do in the coming weeks, but not right now. Keeps the Queue short without losing them.
“Book the car's service” (due next month).
Later
Committed to, but not for a while. Hidden from Focus even if starred.
“Repaint the fence” (next summer).
Someday
Maybe one day, maybe never: ideas and wishes. Look through it in the weekly review and promote or delete things. Hidden from Focus.
“Learn Italian”, “Kayak trip”.
Waiting
Things someone else has to do before you can continue. Fill in Waiting for with who or what, and check the list regularly to chase things up.
“Plumber's quote”, waiting for: Bob's Plumbing (called Mon).
Scheduled
Tasks that can't or shouldn't start until a date (and optional time). They stay out of the way here, then move to the Queue by themselves when the start date (and time) arrives. Adding a future date when you add a task (“tomorrow 3pm”) puts it here, and so does Defer.
“Renew passport” starting 1 March; “Call the bank” tomorrow at 9 am.
Recurring
The next copies of repeating tasks, waiting for their start date. When one arrives it moves to the Queue, and the copy after it appears here. Adding a task while viewing Recurring makes it repeat weekly from tomorrow, and opens it so you can change that.
All
Every open task, grouped by list (except repeating tasks' next copies, which stay in Recurring). Handy for a review or a search across everything.
Projects
Outcomes that take more than one step. See Projects.
Links
Web pages you often need. See Links.
Logbook
Completed tasks, grouped by the day you finished them, newest first, with the time. Untick a task (or right-click → Mark not done) to bring it back. Drag tasks onto Logbook to complete them.
Trash
Deleted tasks, projects and notes. Open one to Restore it or Delete forever; Empty trash deletes them all for good. A project goes to the trash with everything in it (its tasks and subprojects), listed under it; restoring it brings back what went with it, and deleting it deletes all of it. A task restored on its own while its project is still in the trash comes back out of that project.

Adding tasks

Type in the box at the top of a list and press Enter. Ctrl+Enter (⌘+Enter on a Mac) adds it and opens it, to fill in more. Press N anywhere to jump to the box.

The task goes into the list you're looking at: in a project it joins the project's Queue; in Focus it's starred; in Scheduled it starts tomorrow. Anywhere else without a box (like Logbook), N takes you to the Inbox.

Short codes anywhere in the text set things as you type; typing #, !, + or @ offers the choices (↑↓ to choose, Tab or a click to pick).

TypeDoesExample
#tagadds a tag (new tags are created)Buy stamps #errand #post
!listputs it in a list: !inbox, !queue (or !next), !soon, !later, !someday, !waiting, !scheduledQuote from Bob !waiting
!focus or *stars it, for todayCall the school !focus
!Npriority N (−99 to 99)Fix the leak !3, Tidy desk !-1
+areaputs it in an area; the start of the name is enough, and case and spaces don't matterFix shelf +home, Draft spec +side for “Side Projects”
@namenames a contact from your Google Contacts (see Calendar and Google); the start of a name or nickname is enough, and the name stays in the titleCall @mom about Sunday, Lunch with @BobSmith
a date or timesee Dates in plain wordsDentist tomorrow 3pm

All together:

Call mom about Thanksgiving fri 6pm #phone +family !2
→ “Call mom about Thanksgiving”, starts Friday at 6 pm (Scheduled), tag phone, area Family, priority 2.
Renew car registration due nov 30 #car !waiting
→ in Waiting, due 30 November.

A code that doesn't match anything stays in the title (e.g. +garden with no Garden area).

Dates in plain words

Write a date or time naturally and it's taken out of the title. A line under the box shows how it was read (📅 Starts Tomorrow at 3:00 PM · Due Fri…) before you press Enter, so you can rephrase if needed.

  • A date or time on its own is when the task starts. A future start puts the task in Scheduled until then (unless you named a list with !list).
  • After due or by it's the due date. Due tasks show in Focus from that day.
  • today on its own stars the task, for Focus.
  • A time with no date means the next time it comes round (today if it's still ahead, otherwise tomorrow).

Month names need a day next to them (Read May report stays as it is). Only the first date is used; you can always fix it in the task's details.

A task's details

Click a task to open its details right under it, across the list; click the task again (or ✕, or Esc) to close them. Changes save as you go. At the top: the title, which list it's in, and ★ Focus. On the left, notes and files; on the right, where it belongs, When, and More (folded away until you use it).

Title
Start with a verb and make it a visible next step: “Call”, “Email”, “Buy”, “Draft”.
Status
Which list it's in. Choosing Scheduled sets the start date to tomorrow if it has none; change it as needed.
★ Focus
In Focus today (the same as the star on its row, or S).
Waiting for
For a Waiting task: who or what you're waiting on, and since when.
Notes
Anything else: details, phone numbers, links, checklists, with formatting.
Attachments
Files and pictures (see Attachments).
Project
The project it belongs to, if any. A task in a project takes the project's area.
Area
Work, Home… (when it's not in a project).
Tags
Comma-separated; new ones are created.
People
Contacts the task is about (with Google Contacts connected): type to add one. Each shows their phone numbers (tap to call, or Text), email addresses and addresses (opens the map).

When

Starts / at
“Not before”: the day (and optional time) it's for. A start date in the future puts it in Scheduled until then, when it moves to the Queue; clearing it puts it back in the Queue. Shown on Google Calendar when there's no due date.
Due
“Must be done by”: a real deadline. The task shows in Focus from that day, red once overdue.
Remind me
A notification at a time you choose, or a day to three months before the due date; it moves with the due date (see Reminders).
Repeat / Counting from / If missed
See Repeating tasks.

More

Priority
A number; higher comes first in Focus and the Queue.
Time and Energy
How long it takes and how much energy it needs, shown on the row, to help pick a task that fits the moment (“15 min, low energy” for the end of the day).
Blocked by
Other tasks it has to wait for (see Blocked by).
Related to
Other tasks that go with it, both ways, to open from here (see Related to).

More opens by itself when any of these is set, and its heading lists them (“priority 3 · 15m”).

At the bottom: Move to trash (or Restore / Delete forever when it's already in the Trash). Tick the box on a row to complete it.

Notes and formatting

A task's notes, a project's and a note's text are all written the same way, in the same editor: type, and use the buttons above the text (or Ctrl+B, I, U) for bold, italic, underline, strike, a heading, bulleted and numbered lists, a checklist (☐), a quote and a link. The size list (Small, Normal, Large, Larger, Huge) changes the words selected, or, with none selected, what you type next; a note's font list does the same for typefaces. 🖼 puts a picture where the cursor is, 📎 attaches a file, and T̸ clears the formatting of the words selected. Pasting from a web page or document keeps its formatting (what the text can hold). It saves as you type.

  • Checklists: click the box in front of an item to tick it, in the details, on the task's row in a list, in Focus mode, and on a note's card. Enter starts the next item; Enter on an empty one ends the list.
  • Pictures in a task's or project's text work as in a note's: drag them anywhere, click one for its sizes, cropping, turning, fading and Behind text (see Notes). The task's other files are listed under its text, with Put in the text for pictures.
  • In a list, a task's text shows under it, cut short when it's long; open the task to see it all.
  • </> shows the text as its HTML, to edit directly, and back. What the text can't hold (scripts, other tags) is left out when it's saved.
  • Task titles understand a little Markdown: **bold**, *italic*, ~~struck~~, `code` and links.

Vim keys: on a device with a keyboard, Settings → Notes editor → Vim opens text as its HTML (each paragraph, heading and list item on a line of its own) in a box that edits with Vim keys: normal, insert and visual modes, motions and text objects, ., undo (u), / search, registers. :w saves at once (it also saves by itself as you go); :q, :wq or :x closes the task, project or note; :check ticks (or unticks) the checklist item on the cursor's line, and :item starts a new one under it. </> goes to the formatted text and back. It's set per device, so a phone can keep the plain editor.

<p>Packing for the <b>trip</b>:</p>
<ul data-check>
  <li data-done="1">passport</li>
  <li>charger</li>
</ul>

Attachments

Attach receipts, photos, PDFs, documents (any kind of file) to a task in its details:

  • click + Add files, or
  • drag files from your computer onto the open task, or
  • paste an image (e.g. a screenshot) while the task is open.

You can also share to Entropicide from other apps on your phone (with Entropicide installed): photos, files, web pages or text go to your Inbox as a new task, files attached. See Phone app.

Pictures show as thumbnails. Click a picture, PDF or text file to view it full screen without leaving Entropicide: ←/→ (or a swipe on a phone) go to the task's other files, click a picture for its full size, −/+ zoom a PDF, ⤓ downloads, and Esc (or the phone's Back) closes it. Ctrl-click opens a file in a new tab instead; other kinds of file download. ✕ deletes one. The task's row shows 📎 and how many files it has. Up to 25 MB per file and 1 GB in all (Settings → Attachments shows how much you've used, and your largest files); attachments need a connection. Files stay with the task in the Trash and are deleted with it when it's deleted for good. A repeating task's next copies take its files only if you tick that (see Repeating tasks).

Warranty: attach the receipt photo to “Register the dishwasher warranty”.

Notes

Notes (in the sidebar) is a place for anything worth keeping that isn't a task: ideas, recipes, lists, gate codes, meeting notes. Click + New note (or press N), give it a title if you like, and write; it saves as you type. Click a note to open it again.

  • Formatting: the bar over the text does bold, italic, underline and strike (or Ctrl+B/I/U), headings, bulleted and numbered lists, quotes, links (🔗) and clearing the formatting. The font list changes the words selected, or, with none selected, the whole note. Ctrl-click a link in the text to open it.
  • Pictures (🖼, or paste or drop one in) go where the cursor is. Drag a picture to move it anywhere in the text (on a phone, hold it a moment first), or click it for S / M / L sizes, ↑ / ↓ to move it past a paragraph, View, and Remove (it stays with the note's files, and Put in the note brings it back). Other files (📎) are listed under the text.
  • Editing a picture: click it, then Crop to drag a box over the part to keep (the ⟲ / ⟳ buttons there turn it, and Original puts it back as it was added), or ⟳ to turn it a quarter right. The original is kept, so you can change the crop later. The ◐ slider fades it, and Behind text puts it under the words, like paper with a picture on it (faded, so they can be read; click a bare part of it to choose it again). Cropping and turning work on JPEG, PNG and WebP pictures, and need a connection.
  • Drawing (✎ in the toolbar, in a note, task or project): draw a picture where the cursor is.
    • Sketch is pen and ink: six colours, three sizes, an eraser that rubs out whole strokes, undo (Ctrl+Z) and redo. It follows a stylus's pressure, and works with a finger on a phone.
    • Diagram is for boxes, arrows, shapes and words, in a hand-drawn style (it's Excalidraw, built in). It takes a moment to open the first time.
    Either is saved as a picture in the text (sized, moved, faded or put behind the words like any other). Click it, then Edit drawing, to carry on with it. Drawings need a connection to save.
  • Categories each have a colour, which the note shows in. Choose one at the top of the note (or New category…); Edit categories on the Notes page renames them and changes their colours.
  • Tags, an area and Linked to (tasks and projects) are under the text. A task or project shows its notes in its details, with + New note to start one linked to it.
  • 📌 Pin the important ones: they stay at the top.
  • Cards or a list: the Layout choice shows notes as cards (with their text and pictures) or as a list, by category: a heading per category, then a row per note with its first words. A long page draws more as you scroll down, so a big collection stays quick.
  • Right-click a note (press and hold on a phone) to change its category or tags, pin it, make it a waypoint, archive it, make it a task or project, or move it to the trash.
  • Archive a note that's done with but worth keeping (right-click it, or Archive at the bottom of it): it leaves the Notes page but a search still finds it (marked Archived), and Show → Archived lists them all. Unarchive brings one back.
  • ⚑ Waypoints: mark a note as a waypoint (⚑ at the top of it, or right-click) and it shows in a strip across the top of the page, in time order; click one to go to it (see Timelines).
  • Each note shows when it was written and last edited. Sort by last edited, newest, oldest or title.
  • Filter by category (the coloured buttons), by tag (the tags at the top), by area (the sidebar), and to notes that are pinned, or have pictures, files, web links or linked tasks.
  • A task can become a note: right-click it → Make it a note… (or the button in its details). Its notes, tags, files and links go with it. And a note can become a task or a project: Make it a task or Make it a project at the bottom of the note (see Changing what something is).
  • Move to trash in the note; the Trash can restore it.

Searching notes

The search box, on the Notes page, looks through titles, text, tags, categories, file names and linked tasks, ignoring case and accents, and marks what it found. Every word must be there somewhere; Sort: Best match puts the closest first.

Journal

A journal keeps notes at points in time. An entry is something worth saving, with the moment it's about: write as many as you like, on any day, or none for weeks. Journal in the sidebar shows them in order, newest (or oldest) first, under the month; + New entry starts one now. Keep as many journals as you like (a travel journal, a garden log, a work log): + New journal, and the buttons at the top switch between them.

  • An entry is a note with Journal entry ticked: it can have everything a note has (formatting, pictures anywhere in it, a category, tags, links). Beside the tick, choose its journal, its day and its time (now, to begin with; clear the time to leave just the day). Untick it to make it a plain note.
  • On the Notes page, Show chooses notes, journal entries, or both.
  • Click a picture in an entry to see it full size, or the entry to edit it.
  • Deleting a journal moves its entries to the Trash.
  • Entries marked as waypoints (⚑) line up in a strip at the top, to jump through the journal; Timeline shows them on a line through today.
  • Photos shared from your phone can go straight into a journal, each as a new entry at that moment (see Phone app).

Projects

A project is any outcome that needs more than one step: “Kitchen renovation”, “Plan Mom's 70th”, “File 2026 taxes”. Give it a name that says what done looks like, and keep at least one next action in its Queue.

  • Projects in the sidebar opens the Projects page, with every project by status (the number is how many are being worked on: not in Someday or Later, nor completed). Add one with the + beside it. Open a project to see and add its tasks.
  • Move tasks into it with right-click → Move to → Project ›, or the task's Project field.
  • Right-click a project's row (or press and hold it, on a phone) for a menu: its Status (Inbox, Queue, Soon, Waiting, Scheduled, Someday, Later or Completed, one click each, with Undo), Area, Priority, Tag, Open, Details, Star, Sequential and Move to trash.
  • Priority: on the Projects page, press a project's ↕ and drag up to raise its priority, down to lower it, as for tasks (or set it in its details). Higher ones come first, tinted redder; lower ones bluer.
  • Status: a project is in one of the same lists as a task (Queue is what was called Active), or Completed (listed for 30 days). Moving it further off (Soon, Waiting, Scheduled, Someday, Later) takes its tasks and subprojects there too, unless they're further off already; moving it nearer brings back the ones that went with it. A task added to it starts no nearer than it is. Any of them can then be moved nearer by itself, and stays there: park a project in Someday but keep one task in the Queue, say.
    Set Kitchen to Someday: “Measure”, “Pick tiles” and “Get quotes” go to Someday with it.
  • Starts: a project's start date (in its details). One still to come puts it, and what's in it, in Scheduled until then; on the day it all joins the Queue.
  • In the lists: a list's tasks come under their project, and projects show in their list too (a Someday project in Someday, and so on), each above its tasks. Settings → Lists turns that off on a device, leaving each project's name as a heading over its tasks.
  • Sequential: only the first Queue task of the project shows in the Queue and Focus; finishing it brings up the next. Good for steps that must happen in order (“Get quotes” → “Choose builder” → “Sign contract”). Order them by dragging in the project. Its subprojects are steps too, in the same order (drag them among the tasks): when a subproject's turn comes, its tasks show, and the steps after it wait until it's completed.
  • Subprojects: a project can be a part of a bigger one, at any depth: right-click a project → New subproject… or Move into, or choose Part of in its details. On the Projects page they show under their project (▾ folds them away), and the project's page lists them among its tasks (marked ▤), in one order you can change by dragging. Under them, Completed (folded away until you open it) lists the project's finished tasks and subprojects; untick one to bring it back. What holds a project back holds back everything in it: a project in Someday or Later, or waiting on something, keeps all its subprojects' tasks out of the Queue too. A project can't be a part of itself, nor of one that waits on it.
  • A project can have an area, a due date, notes and a star, and, like a task, tags, files, Blocked by and Related to (in its details: Edit project). Its tags count for all its tasks: choosing the tag above a list shows them too. Its files show in each of its tasks' details, under From the project.
  • Convert to project: when a task turns out to need several steps, right-click it (or open it) → Convert to project. The project takes its name, notes, area, due date, priority, star, tags and files, and Someday or Later if it was there. What it was blocked by, the project is blocked by; tasks that waited on it wait on the project; its related tasks are the project's; in a project, it becomes a subproject of it. Tick Make its checklist items the project's tasks to turn its checklist's items into tasks (ticked ones are done). A project has no start date, reminder, time, energy or people, so those go; a repeating task can't be converted.
  • Changing what something is: a task, a project and a note are the same thing underneath (a title, text, an area, tags, files and links), so each can become another and keep all of those: a task → Convert to project or Make it a note; a note → Make it a task or Make it a project (at the bottom of the note); an empty project → Make it a task or Make it a note (right-click it, or its details). What only the old kind had (a note's category, a task's dates) goes. A project with tasks or subprojects in it stays a project until they're moved out.
  • Projects you set up again and again can be saved as templates, and made on a schedule.

Blocked by and Related to

Some tasks can't start until others are done. In a task's details, type in Blocked by and pick the tasks or projects it waits on. Until they're all done, it stays out of the Queue and Focus; elsewhere (All, its project) it shows 🔒 after …. When the last one is done, it turns up in the Queue by itself.

A project can be blocked by tasks and projects too, in its details. While it waits, none of its tasks show in the Queue or Focus. A project counts as done once it's marked complete.

“Paint the bedroom”, blocked by “Buy paint” and “Patch the wall”.
“Send invitations”, blocked by “Book the venue” and “Set the budget”, in another project.

A blocker in the Trash counts as done; ✕ on it takes it off. Nothing can wait in a loop: a project can't wait on anything in it (its tasks and subprojects), nor anything in it on the project. Sequential projects are the simple case: each step waits on the one before.

Related to (under it, in More) links tasks and projects that belong together without either waiting: type and pick, and the link goes both ways. Click a related task to open it; done ones are struck through, and ✕ takes a link off (from both). Rows show 🔗 and how many. Copies (Duplicate, and each copy of a repeating task) keep the links.

“Call the plumber” related to “Kitchen leak”, so either leads to the other, but both stay in the Queue.

Weekly review

Once a week, sit down and bring everything up to date, so you can trust your lists. Review in the sidebar (under Projects) walks you through it, a step at a time; the number beside it is how many projects are due for a look.

  1. Get clear: empty the Inbox (give each task a list or project, or trash it), look over notes written since the last review that have no category, and type anything else on your mind into the box (it goes to the Inbox).
  2. Get current: what's overdue, what you're waiting for (anyone to chase?), and the next two weeks.
  3. Projects: each active project due for review, one at a time, with its tasks and subprojects. ⚠ marks one that's stalled (nothing to do next). Add a next action, then Reviewed, or move it to Someday or Later, or complete it. Each project comes up again after its interval: every week to begin with; 2 or 4 weeks, or never, under Review every (there, or in the project's details).
  4. Get creative: the Someday and Later lists; To the Queue starts one now. New ideas go in the box.
  5. Done: Finish the review notes the day; the page then says when the last one was.

A reminder each week, on the day and at the time you choose: Settings → Notifications → A weekly review reminder. You can also mark a project reviewed any time (right-click it → Mark reviewed).

Inventory

What you have, and where it is. Keep as many inventories as you like (a pantry, food storage, the house); share one with a group (the choice at the top) and everyone in it counts in it.

  • Consumable or kept: each thing is one or the other (Consumable, in its details). Soup, batteries, rice are consumable: counted down as they're used and bought again. Shears, a ladder, the camping stove are kept: they have a place, and can be lent. A food-storage inventory starts things as consumable; a home one, as kept.
  • About each thing: its name and brand (Tomato soup, Campbell's), a category (Canned goods, Paper products, Baking: choose one or type your own), where you usually buy it (Walmart, Amazon) and a link to its page there, its unit (cans, rolls), barcodes, photos, a receipt or manual. By category lists them that way.
  • Counting in: type how many (or leave it at 1), then scan the barcode, or type it and press Enter. 12 cans of soup: 12, scan, done. The count goes back to 1 for the next. − Use counts out the same way. A barcode it doesn't know yet asks once what it is (its name and brand are looked up for you, when they can be found) and where it goes; after that it's known. Type a name instead of a barcode for things without one. − and + on each row count one at a time; Undo puts a count back.
  • Scanning: 📷 Scan uses the camera, and keeps reading, so you can scan can after can. A USB or Bluetooth barcode scanner works too: it types into the box. A thing can have more than one barcode (brands, sizes): add them in its details. New barcodes are looked up at Open Food Facts, a free product database (only the barcode is sent; Settings → Inventory turns it off).
  • Running low: give a consumable thing a Keep at least (and, if you like, how many to buy back up to). Below it, “Buy tomato soup (6 cans)” goes in the inventory's shopping project (shared as the inventory is), and Inventory in the sidebar counts what's low. Ticking the task off asks how many you got and counts them in; counting enough in any other way ticks it off by itself.
  • Places are a tree, as deep as you like: Storage room › Shelf unit 1 › Shelf 1, or Master bedroom › Closet › Dresser › Drawer 6. In Places…, add the rooms, then + Inside on each for what's in it (and Move into… to rearrange); an item's New place… asks what it's inside. Labels prints a sheet of QR labels, one per place: stick them on, and scanning one with a phone's camera opens that place, with what's in it.
  • Lending: a kept thing's Lend it… says who has it; “Get the ladder back from Bob” goes in Waiting. It's back (or ticking that task off) ends it.
  • Things have photos, receipts and manuals (in their details), tags, and go to the Trash like anything else. Search finds them by name, barcode, tag, place, or who has them.

Timelines

A timeline is a line through today: what's done (or written) sits behind it, what's coming (by its due date, else its start date) ahead of it. Not everything goes on it, just what you mark as a waypoint (⚑): the milestones.

  • Mark a waypoint: right-click a task, project or note → Waypoint, or ⚑ in a task's details, a project's details or a note. Waypoints show ⚑ on their rows.
  • Timeline in the sidebar shows the waypoints of all your tasks and projects; a project's Timeline (beside Edit project) just what's in it, subprojects too; a journal's Timeline its entries.
  • Show → Everything dated fills the timeline in with everything that has a date, waypoint or not.
  • Click an item to open it. Done tasks open in the Logbook.
  • On the Notes and Journal pages, waypoints line up in a strip across the top; click one to go to it.
A move: “Sign the lease” (done), “Book the van” (due Friday), “Moving day” (due the 30th) as waypoints of the project; the boxes to pack stay off the timeline.

Templates and recurring projects

For things you do again and again: a trip, hosting a dinner, the start of school, the monthly bills.

Save a template

Set up a project once (its tasks, notes, checklists, tags, priorities, who's blocked by what, and dates), then in its details click Save as template. Dates are kept as days from the project's date (its due date, or else its earliest task date): “Book the kennel 14 days before”, “Pack the day before”. Checklists are kept unticked.

Use it

+ next to Projects (or New project on a template under Projects): choose the template, name the project, and give its date (say, the day you leave). The tasks get their dates from it, and ones that can't start yet wait in Scheduled until their day.

Template “Trip”: Book kennel (starts −14), Hold the mail (−3), Pack (due −1), Leave (blocked by Pack). New project from it, “Trip to Denver” on Dec 20: the kennel task shows up in the Queue on Dec 6.

Edit it

Under Projects → Templates, Edit: rename it, change its tasks' days, add or remove tasks. For more (notes, tags, what waits on what), make a project from it, change that, then click Update template in the project's details.

Recurring projects

In the template editor, under Repeat, have it make a new project every week, month, year or N days. Give the next date (the project's date) and how many days ahead to make it, so early tasks have time. Each one is named with its date, e.g. Monthly bills (Nov 1). If Entropicide isn't opened for a while, only the latest is made, not every one that was missed.

“Monthly bills”, every month from Nov 1, made 5 days ahead: Pay rent (due 0), Check the bank (starts −2), File the receipts (starts +3).

Repeating tasks are still best for single things; recurring projects are for a set of steps.

Areas

Areas are the big parts of your life: Work, Home, Family, Side Projects. They let you see only one part at a time.

  • The area filter at the top of the sidebar: tick one or several areas (or No area) to show only those, across every list. Untick all (or Show all areas) to see everything. It's remembered on each device.
    At work: tick Work. At home in the evening: tick Home and Family.
  • New tasks and projects go in the first ticked area, so they stay in view.
  • Create, rename and delete areas in Settings → Areas. Give each an SVG icon (upload a file, or paste a URL) and it shows before its tasks.

Tags and search

  • Tags are for anything that cuts across lists and areas: where (#errand, #hardware-store), how (#phone, #computer), with whom (#bob), or what kind (#quick).
  • The tag bar under the add box shows the tags used in the current list; click tags to show only tasks that have all of them. Click again to turn one off.
    Going out? In the Queue, click errand to see everything you can do while out.
  • Search (top right, or press /) filters the current list; use the All list to search everything. It works as on the Notes page: tasks with all the words, anywhere (title, text, tags, project, area, files, people), "a phrase", -word to leave one out, and:
    #tag or tag:, title:, project:, area:just there (project:none, area:none for none)
    in:queue, in:someday…in that list
    is:focus, blocked, waiting, overdue, due, today, repeating, scheduledwhat it is
    has:file, picture, link, checklist, reminder, people, related, notewhat it has
    before:, after:, on: a date (2026-11-20, 2026-11, today, tomorrow)by its due date, else its start date
    #errand -hardware is:overdue · project:house has:checklist · in:someday "trip"
    Start with a slash for a regular expression instead: /^call (mum|dad)/, /\d{3}-\d{4}/, /receipt.*2026/. It ignores case unless you type a capital letter; the box turns red (and says why when you point at it) if the pattern isn't valid. It works on the Links page too.
  • Rename, merge (rename to an existing tag) and delete tags in Settings → Tags.

Priority

Priority is a number, 0 by default. Focus and the Queue list higher numbers first; within the same number, your drag order. Negative numbers sink a task below the rest.

  • Press and hold the ↕ at the end of a task's row and drag up to raise the priority, down to lower it (the new number shows as you go; it's set when you let go). Or press +/− on the open or selected tasks, right-click → Priority → Increase / Decrease / Reset to 0, use the Priority field, or !N when adding.
  • Dragging a task within Focus or the Queue gives it the priority of where you drop it, so it stays where you put it.
  • Colour: tasks above 0 are tinted red, deeper the higher the number; below 0, blue, deeper the lower. Priority 0 has no tint.
Keep most tasks at 0 and give only the few that really matter 1–3; use −1 for “if there's time”.

Several tasks at once

  • Select: Ctrl/⌘-click tasks to pick them; Shift-click picks a range; Ctrl/⌘+A picks every task shown. On a phone, press and hold a task → Select, then tap others. Esc clears the selection.
  • Act: right-click any picked task (or Actions in the bar above the list) to Move to, Area, Priority, Tag, Defer, Star, Complete, Duplicate or Move to trash all of them. Move to and Area are rows of buttons at the top of the menu: one click moves them (Project › lists your projects). Each change can be undone from the message that follows.
  • Drag them together: onto a list, Focus (stars them), Logbook (completes them) or Trash.
  • Right-click (or press and hold) a single task for the same menu. Duplicate makes a copy just below it (same details, tags, files and blockers; a repeating task's copy repeats the same way, on its own schedule) and opens it, ready to change.
Weekly review: in the Inbox, Ctrl+A, right-click → Area → Home, then drag the work ones to Work.

Defer

Defer hides a task until later: right-click → Defer and pick a time. The task goes to Scheduled and comes back to the Queue when the time arrives. Hours and minutes defer to an exact time; days, weeks and months to the start of that day. Custom… picks any date and time.

The choices are set in Settings → Defer options, e.g. 1h, 2h, 6h, 24h, 3d, 1w (m = minutes, h = hours, d = days, w = weeks, mo = months).

Can't call the office until it opens? Defer 2h. Not a priority this week? Defer 1w.

Repeating tasks

Set Repeat in a task's details: daily, weekdays, weekly, monthly, yearly, on certain days of the week, on a weekday of the month (the first Monday, the last Friday), or every N days. The schedule follows the calendar, not when you complete it: each copy starts on its date, and when it does, the next copy appears in Recurring.

If missed decides what happens to an unfinished copy when the next one starts:

  • Keep it: it stays open alongside the new one. For things that must still be done.
    “Register the van”, yearly, keep: if it's late, it still has to happen.
  • Replace it: it's logged as Missed and the new copy takes over. For routines where only the latest counts.
    “Load the dishwasher”, daily, replace.

Every N days, weeks, months or years: choose Custom… and type e.g. 9 days, 2 weeks, 3 months or 1 year. Month repeats keep to the same day of the month (the 31st becomes the last day in shorter months).

On certain days of the week (Mon, Wed and Fri, say): choose On certain days of the week…, then switch days on and off with the buttons under it. Each copy is on the next of those days.

A weekday of the month (the first Monday, the second Tuesday, the last Friday): choose Monthly on a weekday…; it starts as the one the task's date is, and the two menus under it change it. Missed copies are kept, as with other monthly repeats.

When adding: every day, every weekday, every week, every other week, every 6 weeks, every month, every 3 months, every year, every 9 days, every monday (weekly, from the next Monday), or several days, every mon, wed and fri, or a weekday of the month, every first monday, every 2nd tue, every last friday of the month: Haircut every 6 weeks, Gym every mon wed fri 7am.

Counting from: by default a repeat follows the calendar (every Monday, every 9 days from the start, whether or not the last one was done). Choose When I complete it instead for things where the gap matters: nothing is made on a schedule; when you complete it, the next copy is made that far from the day you did it (and If missed no longer applies).

“Change the water filter”, every 3 months, counting from when I complete it: done late on May 20, the next is due Aug 20, not on the old schedule.
“Refill pill containers”, every 9 days (replace if missed). If a refill happens early or late, change the start date of the newest copy: the schedule carries on every 9 days from there.

Each copy starts fresh: checklist items ([x]) are unticked on every new copy, so a weekly cleaning list or a packing list can be ticked off again. Tick Give each new copy this task's files to have its attachments (say, a printable checklist) on every copy too; they're stored only once.

Editing the newest copy's dates changes the schedule from then on; editing an older copy moves only that one. Deferring a copy moves only that copy. To stop repeating, set Repeat to Never (or trash every copy).

Reminders and notifications

Entropicide can send notifications to your phone or computer. Turn them on in Settings → Notifications, on each device (Turn on for this device; Send a test to check). Then you get:

  • Reminders: set Remind me in a task's details, under its due date: At a time… for any date and time, or (when it has a due date) from 1 day to 3 months before it's due, at 9 am. Or click In 1 hour, Tonight (7 pm) or Tomorrow 9 am. The task shows ⏰ and the time until then. A reminder moves with the due date: push the deadline back a week and the reminder goes a week later too (one set before it's due is simply worked out again). A notification for one set before it's due says how long is left (“⏰ Due in 30 days: Renew passport”). On a repeating task, each new copy gets the reminder too, at the same distance from its own date: “Remind me Jan 14, 9 am” on a copy starting Jan 1 becomes Jul 14, 9 am on the next one (every 6 months). Setting or clearing it updates copies already waiting in Recurring; snoozing a notification moves only that copy.
  • Starts: when a task's start time comes (e.g. Call the bank tomorrow 9am), if When a timed task starts is ticked.
  • A morning summary of what's due and overdue, and how many are starred, at the time you choose.

Act on it from the notification itself: ✓ Done completes the task (a repeating one's next copy appears as usual), and Snooze 1 hour brings it back later (phones show two buttons; computers may add Tomorrow). If that can't reach Entropicide (no connection), the notification says so. Tapping the notification opens the task.

Quiet hours (Settings → Notifications): nothing buzzes between the two times you choose, e.g. 10 pm to 7 am. Reminders and starts that fall in them aren't lost: they all arrive when the quiet hours end.

“Take the bins out” · Remind me: Tonight → at 7 pm your phone buzzes; snooze an hour if you're busy.

On an iPhone or iPad, notifications work once Entropicide is added to the Home Screen and opened from there. With the Android app, allow notifications when Android asks.

Focus timer

To work on one task without distractions: ▶ Focus timer in its details, right-click → Focus timer, or press T on it. Choose 15, 25, 45 or 60 minutes (or no limit); the task fills the screen with a countdown, its notes and its tickable checklist, and the screen stays on.

  • Pause (or Space), ✓ Done (completes the task), or Stop.
  • Esc (or ⌄) shrinks it to a small timer in the corner; click that to come back. It carries on through a reload.
  • When time's up you hear a short chime (and get a notification if Entropicide isn't on screen); then +5 min, another round, complete, or stop.
  • The time is added to the task: ⏱ on its row and Time spent in its details (with Reset).

Calendar and Google

  • The calendar icon in the toolbar shows the next two weeks: your dated tasks and your Google Calendar events.
  • Focus starts with your calendar: Today (events still to come or going on now) beside Tomorrow, one line each with its time first (point at one for its end time; click it to open it in Google Calendar). Each of today's leaves once it has ended; all-day events stay all day. With more than one calendar, each says which it's from. − on an event makes it small and faint: still there, but not one to focus on. + beside Today or Tomorrow brings that day's back to full size. (Kept with your account, so every device shows the same: one that's open catches up within half a minute.) The weather shows beside them: what it's like now, and tomorrow's high and low. Point at either (or tap it, on a phone) for the week: each day's weather, high and low, and chance of rain or snow. Say where in Settings → Weather (search for your town, or use your location), in °F or °C. It comes from Open-Meteo, a free weather service, asked by this server (only the place is sent).
  • Settings → Google Calendar: connect your Google account and choose a calendar. Tasks with a due date (or else a start date) appear on it as events: a shared one like Family so everyone sees them, or a new Entropicide calendar.
  • Sync runs both ways every minute: move or rename the event in Google and the task follows; delete it and the task goes to the Trash. On your own Entropicide calendar, events you add there become tasks. On a shared calendar, other people's events are left alone.
  • Contacts: the same connection keeps a read-only copy of your Google Contacts (refreshed every hour), so a task can name people: @Mom in quick add, or People in its details. Their name shows on the task's row, and its details have buttons to call, text, email or map them. Connected before Contacts was added? Click Reconnect in Settings and tick the box for Google Contacts.
  • Today's events also show at the top of Focus. Sync now in Settings syncs straight away; Disconnect removes the events Entropicide added.

Links and the toolbar

  • The Links list keeps web pages you use often: paste a URL, give it a name, file it in folders (drag to rearrange, or drop on a folder to move it in).
  • Star a link to put it in the toolbar across the top of every list, for one-click access.
  • Give a link an SVG icon (✎ → Upload icon or From URL) and the toolbar shows just the icon, saving space.
Star Mail, Bank and School portal with icons; keep the rest in folders like “Bills” and “Kids”.

Phone app and offline

  • Install the app: an Android download, or Add to Home Screen from the browser on any phone or computer. It's the same site, full screen, always up to date.
  • Share to Entropicide: once installed on Android, Entropicide is in the Share menu of other apps. Share a web page, some text, a photo or a file and it becomes a task in your Inbox (the page's link and text in its notes, files attached), and Entropicide opens on it. Or have shares go to Notes as a new note, or to a new entry in a journal (Settings → Sharing to Entropicide); pictures are placed in the text, where you can drag them about. Choose Ask each time there and Entropicide asks where each share should go: a task in the Inbox, a new note, or a new entry in one of your journals (closing the question leaves it in the Inbox).
    In Chrome: ⋮ → Share → Entropicide, to keep an article for later. In Photos: Share → Entropicide, to keep a receipt.
    If a share doesn't arrive, look in Settings → Troubleshooting → App log: it lists every share the server received and what became of it.
  • Add without leaving what you're doing (Android app 1.0.4 or later): in the app, open Settings → Add from the phone and tap Connect this phone (allow notifications if asked).
    • A quiet Add a task notification stays in the pull-down panel: tap Add task, type, and send, as when replying to a text. The game or app you're in keeps running.
    • Or add the Add task tile to Quick Settings (swipe down twice, tap ✏️, drag it in): it asks in a small box over whatever's on screen.
    The first line is read just like quick add (Call dentist tomorrow 3pm #phone !next), any more lines become the notes, and it goes to the Inbox unless you name a list. The notification says what happened (Added: Call dentist · Queue · Mon Nov 2, 3:00 PM · #phone, or why not). With no signal, it waits on the phone (1 task waiting for a connection) and sends itself when you're back online, with its dates read from when you typed it. Swiped it away? Opening Entropicide brings it back; to stop it for good, Disconnect the phone in Settings (the notification goes the next time it's used), or turn off its notifications in Android's settings for Entropicide.
  • Groups (a family, a club): people with an account on this Entropicide who will share things. Settings → Groups → make one, then invite people by their username; they join from their own Settings → Groups (and get a notification, if they've turned them on). Each person is an owner (invites people, sets roles, ends the group), an editor (changes what's shared) or a viewer (sees it; and, if the owner allows, ticks off tasks assigned to them). New people join as sets the role an invitation starts with. Anyone can leave; a group always keeps an owner, and ends when the last person leaves.
  • Sharing with a group:
    • A project (its details → Shared with): it and everything in it, including what anyone adds to it later.
    • A journal (the choice beside its Timeline): every entry, whoever writes it; each shows who wrote it.
    • A single task or note (its details, or the choice at the top of a note).
    What's shared shows 👥 (and “by Ann” when someone else made it); 👥 Shared in the sidebar lists it all. Editors and owners can change it; viewers can only look. Only whoever made something can share it, or stop sharing it. Moving something out of a shared project makes it yours alone (it asks first). Ending a group gives everything back to whoever made it (what was in a project, to whoever made the project).
  • Who does a shared task: its For (in its details) is someone in the group (it's in their lists alone, and they get a notification; reminders go to them) or anyone (it's in everyone's lists, and whoever ticks it off has done it). Your star, area and place in the order of a shared task are your own, as is a shared note's pin and category.
  • Seeing others' changes: the app checks every 30 seconds (and when you come back to it) and shows what's changed, unless you're in the middle of typing. If two people change a shared note's text at once, both are kept: yours, and theirs as a note of its own, “… (the other version)”.
  • Add from other apps (Alexa, Home Assistant, a phone shortcut, a script): in Settings → Add from other apps, name what it's for and click Make a key. Copy the key straight away (it's shown once). The other app then sends the task's text to the address shown there, with the key, and it's read just like quick add: Buy milk tomorrow #errand. Tick Can also read my lists for a key that may also fetch Focus, the Queue or another list (for "Alexa, ask … what's due today?"), and Can also complete tasks for one that may tick them off by name ("… I finished call the dentist"). Revoke stops a key working. Each add appears in the App log. Setting one up (with examples for Alexa, Home Assistant and iPhone Shortcuts) is in docs/api.md.
  • App updates: Entropicide itself is always current. When the Android app around it changes, opening it shows A new version of the Entropicide app is ready: tap Update, open the download, and tap Update. Later skips that version. Settings → Troubleshooting shows which version you're running (1.0.1, 1.0.2, …) and whether a newer one is out.
  • Offline: once it has been opened on a device, it opens without a connection and shows your tasks as last loaded. You can keep adding, editing, completing, moving and trashing tasks and projects; a badge at the top counts the changes waiting, and they're sent when the connection is back (“Back online. Your changes are saved.”). Settings, the Logbook and the Trash need a connection.

A daily and weekly routine

Each day (5 minutes)

  1. Empty the Inbox: for each item, do it if it takes under 2 minutes, otherwise move it to a list or project (or trash it).
  2. Look at Focus (due today, today's events) and star a few Queue tasks for today.
  3. Work from Focus. Capture anything new straight into the Inbox.

Each week (30 minutes)

  1. Empty the Inbox (and your email, notes and pockets into it).
  2. Go through Projects: does each active one have a next action in the Queue? Is any finished, or better parked in Someday?
  3. Check Waiting: chase anything overdue.
  4. Look ahead at Scheduled, Recurring and the Calendar.
  5. Skim Soon, Later and Someday: promote what's now relevant, delete what isn't.
  6. Trim the Queue: reprioritise; move what you won't get to soon into Soon or Later.
